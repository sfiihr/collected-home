Designing a Home that Feels Collected, not Decorated

Since we moved into our builder-grade home in 2017, my design style has evolved significantly. What started as a focus on building a coordinated space that resembled a model home has turned into a journey to create a space that is much more personal: spaces that feel collected over time, reflect the people who live in them, and don't avoid a little personality in the process.

A Builder-Grade Beginning

We chose our home in its final stages of construction, so we had zero input on the design elements. Cabinets, countertops, backsplash, floors, and nearly every other finish had already been chosen, ordered, and were awaiting installation. For me, personally, this was my nightmare - black, white, and gray felt more like a hospital or a prison than a new home. What many people would embrace with open arms was something I needed to change as quickly as possible.

And Then There was Green...

As I set my sights on the kitchen, the zellige-style trend was just entering retailers. Walking into a local tile shop, I spotted her: ceramic, zellige-look tile, in a deep, rich, variegated green. JACKPOT. I immediately had a vision. Cabinets could stay, counters would go lighter, we'd do funky herringbone layout because green wasn't quite weird enough, throw in some gold hardware and accents, et voila! A kitchen was born. And BORN was it ever. I was in love - it was a little weird, it felt perfectly coordinated and complete, and I was ready to move onto my next space... until it was time to start piecing that green, open-concept vision together.

Kermit was Right - It's not Easy Being Green

A green backsplash was a bold choice, and it felt like the right one when juxtaposed against my monochromatic, builder-grade space. What I didn't anticipate was just how demanding that particular shade of green would become when I began to design the spaces that touched it.

Because the kitchen was a centerpiece in our open floor plan, every decision started with the same question: Does that work with the green? I am embarrassed to admit that trying to answer this question cost me the price of multiple rugs, curtain choices, accent chairs, throw pillows, and even Christmas decorations before I realized there had to be another way.

From Coordinated to Collected

Nearly a decade in, after taking time to live in and feel the space, I've embarked on a new journey. Over these last ten years, I've had the privilege of inheriting mementos and heirlooms from my parents and grandparents, from photos to paintings to objects they loved. Not everything I receive suits my style, but the things that do have turned out to be things that don't necessarily match an aesthttic - they're the things with stories, memories I've heard of, memories I've made, and pieces of the people who have touched my life the most. These are the pieces that I'm now using to inspire the next chapter of our home - one we live in, not just look at. A collected space that reflects the history of our lives.

Partnering with an interior designer who took time to understand the pieces I had and loved, the pieces I loved and wanted, and who heard our goals for our home was a game-changer. My designer didn't build a vision from scratch - she largely helped me piece together our collection with pieces I had already found and "pinned" as inspiration. She was mindful of the personal mementos, art, and objects that would continue to fill my space, and she left room for me to personalize. Partnering with a designer doesn't mean you lack inspiration or vision - I've found that, if you have the right level of discussion and find someone that hears you, they become the expert that harnesses your inspiration and turns it into a plan that may be better than you had ever imagined.

The end result is a work in progress. There are pieces to purchase, recommendations to swap with more personal touches, and if I had to guess, plenty of adjustments along the way. But that's the point of a collected home - never static, always evolving, and perfectly imperfect.
